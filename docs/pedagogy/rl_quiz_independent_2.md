# Independent RL Assessment: AlphaZero-style Chess (Quiz 3)

Answer each question in 2-5 sentences. If the material you studied does not teach something needed to answer, write NOT COVERED rather than using outside knowledge.

Questions marked [T] are transfer questions: they describe a situation you have probably not seen and ask you to reason about it.

---

**Q1. The formalism.** Describe chess self-play as a reinforcement learning problem: what are the states, actions, rewards and transitions, and what quantity is the agent ultimately trying to maximise? Explain why a reward that arrives only at the end of the game, with no discounting, is still a well-defined objective here, and how the two-player, zero-sum nature of the game shows up in how values are stored and backed up during search.

**Q2. Why self-play improves.** In this engine the policy head is trained towards the visit-count distribution produced by PUCT search, not towards the move that was actually played, and the value head is trained towards the final game result. Explain why training the network to imitate its own search can make the network *stronger* rather than just reproducing itself, and name the condition that must hold for this loop to keep improving rather than stall.

**Q3. [T] Monte Carlo vs bootstrapped value targets.** The value target is currently the final game outcome z (+1/0/-1 from the side to move). A colleague proposes replacing it with the search's own root value estimate at that position (the average backed-up Q after the simulations), "because it is less noisy than a single game result." Compare the two targets in terms of bias and variance. Predict what would happen if you made this switch right at the start of training from random weights, and suggest a compromise.

**Q4. [T] The move cap and a sparse reward.** Self-play games longer than `max_moves` plies are stopped and scored as a draw. Early in training, from random weights, suppose almost every game hits the cap. What does the value head learn from this data, and why does that also damage the policy targets? A teammate suggests scoring capped games by material balance instead of as draws. Give one benefit and one risk of that change in RL terms.

**Q5. [T] Exploration.** Self-play uses Dirichlet noise at the search root and temperature 1 move selection for the first 20 plies, while arena games use neither. (a) Predict what happens to the replay buffer and to learning over many iterations if both exploration mechanisms are switched off in self-play. (b) Explain why the policy training target is taken at temperature 1 (raw visit proportions) even on moves where the move actually played was chosen greedily. (c) Why is it correct to switch exploration off in the arena?

**Q6. Off-policy data.** The replay buffer holds positions generated by several past champions, and the moves in those games were partly chosen by noise and temperature. For the value target z, say precisely *which* policy's value z is an estimate of, and why that is not exactly the value of the current greedy player. Explain why the buffer is a finite window rather than "keep everything", and what goes wrong at each extreme of buffer size.

**Q7. [T] Stability and the gate.** In `train_loop.py`, each iteration the candidate is a fresh copy of the champion, trained for `train_steps` steps, and is discarded if it fails the arena gate. Suppose you run with very few `train_steps` per iteration and see the candidate rejected ten iterations in a row. Explain why the loop might be stuck *by construction*, not because learning is impossible, and propose two changes. Then explain what problem the gate is protecting against in the first place, in terms of the network generating its own training data.

**Q8. [T] Credit assignment.** In one self-play game White blunders a rook on move 30, Black fails to notice, and White eventually wins. Every White-to-move position in that game, including the blunder position, is labelled +1. Explain why this does not permanently teach the network that the blunder is good, which parts of the system supply finer-grained credit than the game result, and what would make this kind of mislabelling more damaging.

**Q9. [T] Honest evaluation.** The arena plays 6 games, alternating colours, with no root noise and temperature 0, and promotes the candidate if it scores above 55%. (a) Given that both networks are deterministic at evaluation time, how many genuinely *distinct* games does this match actually contain, and what does that do to the reliability of the score? (b) Even with a fixed design, explain why "beats the previous champion" is not on its own good evidence that the agent is getting stronger in general. (c) Propose a better evaluation protocol.

**Q10. [T] Roles of policy and value.** Inside PUCT, the policy head and value head do different jobs. Predict how search behaves, and what the resulting engine would be weak at, in each of these sabotaged cases: (a) the value head is replaced by a function that always returns 0 but the policy head is well trained; (b) the policy head is replaced by uniform priors but the value head is well trained. Then explain why having a value head is what allows PUCT to dispense with random rollouts.
